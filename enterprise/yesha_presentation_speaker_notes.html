4b · NEW: the agent proposes its own redline (the headline since we last met)

"Last time you asked whether it could go further than flagging — whether it could propose the edit. As of today it does."
Do In the same chat as the original-DSA run (Input C1), type: "Now give me the redline / proposed edits for this contract."
Point at The Suggested edits table: for each clause, the original language with its cite, the issue, and a concrete insert / delete / replace — e.g., restoring the PCI, indemnification, and liability-cap protections, tightening "commercially reasonable," putting the 5-day deadline back. Note the header: "draft, not final/binding language", routed to Contracting/Legal.
Say the guardrail out loud "It proposes the edit — it never changes the contract and never approves it. Where our playbook has no language, it says so and marks it 'Legal to draft.' That line — propose, don't decide — is deliberate, and it's a governance choice, not a technical limit."

If asked — quick answers

  • Premium license? No — shared agents need none; Copilot app or m365.cloud.microsoft, not Word.
  • What model? GPT-4-class via Microsoft/OpenAI, unconfirmed and changeable; tracking with Brian.
  • Does it edit the document? It proposes edits (the new redline mode — a suggested insert/delete/replace per clause) but never changes the contract itself or approves it; every edit is marked draft and routed to Legal. The linked view shows notes beside the clause without touching the source.
  • 100-page contract? Reviews in full and is honest about coverage; a documented section-by-section workflow for oversized ones, pairing with Shivam's long-doc work.
  • Accuracy? Every finding cites the clause (with a verbatim anchor) and the playbook rule; it re-checks its own citations; validated on planted-trap documents.
  • Is the output too long? It's thorough and all cited/searchable; a concise mode is easy to add — best decided with the pilot reviewer.
