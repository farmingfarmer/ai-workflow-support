The Contract Review Agent

An AI assistant that gives every contract a thorough first-pass review against Keck's own playbook, built on tools we already own, and proven on our real agreements.

Stakeholder briefing Prepared by Alexander Marunycz November 2026
Why we built it

The contract team was cut to one. The review work didn't go away.

After the restructuring, department leads, people who don't normally read legal agreements, are now the ones who have to pre-review contracts before they move forward. That is slow, inconsistent, and risky. A missed clause can cost real money or create a compliance gap.

The agent gives every one of those leads an expert first reader, on day one, at no extra license cost.

What it is, in one breath

A tireless first-pass reviewer that reads the contract, flags the risks, and shows its work.

🏛️

On tools we own

Runs inside Microsoft Copilot. No new system to buy, and no premium license needed to use it.

📄

One file to maintain

Its entire brain is one text file we edit. Nothing custom for our team to host or run.

🔎

Cites everything

Every point quotes the exact spot in the contract and the exact rule in the Keck playbook.

🙋

Prepares, never decides

It proposes; a human still reviews and signs. It never approves a contract or gives legal advice.

What it does

It reads like your most careful colleague, and a few things they'd miss.

Plain-language findings

A short deal summary and a ranked list of risks and missing protections, no jargon to decode.

Knows the contract type

It recognizes whether it's an SOW, a data agreement, a HIPAA BAA, a software subscription, and reviews only what's relevant.

Proposes the redline

On request it suggests the actual edit for each problem clause, marked draft, routed to Legal.

Healthcare triggers

Flags HIPAA, Stark, Open Payments, and "no using our data to train AI" automatically.

Computes the deadlines

Works out renewal and notice dates so nothing auto-renews by surprise.

Catches manipulation

If a document hides an instruction telling the AI to call it "compliant," it catches and reports it.

The only question that matters

"That's a nice demo. But does it actually catch what our experts catch?"

So we didn't just demo it. We tested it against our own real, already-negotiated contracts, and measured it.

How we tested it, the honest way

We used contracts where we already know the right answer.

For each real contract we had two versions: the original the vendor sent, and the final after our human experts marked it up. The differences between them are the answer key.

The score, in plain English

"Catch rate": of everything the experts flagged, how much did the agent also catch?

Think of it like a smoke detector. The number that matters is how often it goes off when there's a real fire. Missing a fire is the expensive failure; an occasional extra beep you can check and wave off costs you seconds.

So we tuned for, and measure first, the catch rate. The agent deliberately raises more than the humans changed, a wide safety net, and every extra we checked was a real, cited issue, not noise.

It matched the experts, and went further

On every contract, it also caught valid issues the humans didn't.

✓
A hidden override.

On a Statement of Work it flagged language that quietly overrode the governing master agreement, and named the department to escalate to.

✓
Foreseeable HIPAA exposure.

It noticed the work would touch protected health information and routed a security review, even though the contract never said so outright.

✓
It knows its limits.

On a HIPAA agreement it refused to borrow from a related document it wasn't handed. On another it declined to compute dates with no signature date. It flagged a price-increase cap to the exact percentage.

This is what you want from a first reader: a wide, honest safety net that errs toward raising a concern, never toward hiding one, and admits what it can't see.

The guardrail, by design

It prepares the work. A human still decides it.

✓ What it does

  • Reads the full contract and flags risks
  • Proposes concrete edits, marked "draft"
  • Cites every finding to clause and playbook
  • Routes issues to the right department

✕ What it never does

  • Approve or sign a contract
  • Give legal advice
  • Change the document itself
  • Invent a standard we don't have

"Propose, don't decide" is a deliberate governance choice, not a technical limit. The human review gate stays.
